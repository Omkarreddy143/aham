# AHAM judging speaking script

Confirmed Tattva: **"Consciousness is beyond the body and mind. Show artificial sense of Self in body and mind."** Use these sections as modules; no presentation duration is assumed. [Preparation and Q&A](judging-preparation.md), [shlokas and explanation](tattva-explanation.md).

## Teammate 1 — opening

"Our Tattva is consciousness beyond the body and mind. We approach it through a simple question: what makes a hand feel like 'my hand,' and what is aware of that feeling?

"AHAM creates an artificial hand representation. It follows a real person's movement and lets them interact with a virtual world. We use that experience to distinguish the body that is represented, the mind's sense of 'mine,' and awareness noticing both."

## Teammate 2 — the build and confirmed status

"Quest estimates finger joints and wrist pose. Our WebXR application moves the virtual hand and detects contact and grasping. It calculates five vibration cues and five resistance requests. A laptop sends those values over hotspot Wi-Fi to NodeMCU.

"The confirmed working part is VR tracking and actual ESP receipt. Physical vibration and servo resistance are not yet verified. We will show requests without presenting them as physical sensations.

"Our contribution is this tracking, interaction and wireless feedback architecture. The prepared hardware stage can later add verified tactile output without rebuilding the hand-tracking system."

## Teammate 3 — guide the experience

Place **BODY / MIND / AWARENESS** labels beside the laptop.

Say: "Open your right hand and turn your wrist. Bend a finger. Touch this core, pick it up, lift it and open your hand over its matching dock."

Point to **BODY** while the live hand moves. Teammate 2 shows a changing request and its return to zero on release. State once that no physical motor cue is produced.

Ask: "What did the hand feel like to you?" Let the participant answer without suggesting ownership. Then ask: "Could you control it? Did it feel like part of your body, or an object you controlled?"

Point to **MIND**, using only their report. If they report control only, say: "You described control without ownership. Those are different outcomes."

Ask: "Can you notice the hand and also the thought or feeling you have about it?" Point to **AWARENESS**.

Optionally take the hand out of camera view and return it. Explain that disappearance of a representation does not mean the real person disappeared.

## Teammate 4 — explain the Tattva

"Control is agency: 'I caused that movement.' Ownership is the feeling 'this belongs to my body.' Movement correspondence is one condition studied in virtual body-ownership research. We ask for the participant's experience rather than assuming it.

"The body representation here is artificial, but the participant may identify with it. That makes the sense of 'mine' available for reflection. It is an artificial route to an experience in a person; it does not make the computer conscious.

"The Tattva takes the reflection further. The hand changes position. The mind's thought may change from 'that is a model' to 'that feels like mine,' or it may not. The participant can notice both the hand and the thought. In the Tattva's terms, these are contents known in awareness; consciousness is not equated with one of those contents.

"The Bhagavad Gita distinguishes the body as the field and its knower as the knower of the field. We use that distinction to explain our official theme: the represented body is observed, the thought of ownership is observed, and we ask about awareness of both.

"The original brief compares feeling affected by a virtual-hand event with identifying ourselves with bodily pleasure and pain. We distinguish an event, a sensation, an interpretation, and awareness of it. Since physical haptics are unfinished, our live reflection begins with movement and identification.

"This is an experiential illustration. It does not scientifically prove consciousness independent of the physical body or settle its nature. The philosophical claim belongs to the theme; the prototype makes its question visible."

If comfortable, recite the main verse and explain it immediately:

> इदं शरीरं कौन्तेय क्षेत्रमित्यभिधीयते।
> एतद्यो वेत्ति तं प्राहुः क्षेत्रज्ञ इति तद्विदः॥

Our paraphrase: **the body is the field; its knower is distinguished from that field.** [Bhagavad Gita 13.2](https://www.holy-bhagavad-gita.org/chapter/13/verse/2/). Pronunciation and the optional supporting verse are in the shloka guide. Recitation without explanation is insufficient.

## Teammate 1 — impact and close

"Our immediate purpose is experiential education. The participant performs a task, describes control and ownership, then reflects on awareness of the experience. We assess usability separately from ownership and conceptual understanding.

"Our roadmap adds verified contact cues and bounded mechanical resistance. We do not claim medical benefit, real object weight or consciousness inside electronics.

"AHAM makes a representation respond to a person. It may feel like 'mine.' The Tattva asks us to look beyond the representation and thought of ownership, toward the consciousness aware of both."

## Core explanation everyone should remember

"We create an artificial hand that moves with the user. The user may experience agency or ownership. The feeling of 'my hand' is itself something they can notice. Our Tattva distinguishes the body and the mind's identification from consciousness aware of them. AHAM illustrates that reflection; it does not create or measure consciousness in the ESP."

If ownership is absent, say: "This participant reported control without ownership. That is valid. We can still examine the representation, their thought about it and awareness of both."

If the network fails, use a genuine labeled recording. A synthetic desktop rehearsal shows scene behavior, not live Quest receipt, physical touch or a participant's ownership experience.

## The message to remember

**AHAM creates an artificial body representation and explores how it becomes "my hand" in the participant's experience. The Tattva asks us to distinguish that representation, and the mind's identification with it, from the consciousness aware of both.**

Tracking supplies the connection between intention and visible movement. Physical feedback, once verified, supplies a corresponding bodily cue. The Tattva connection is the identification through which an event involving a hand is experienced as affecting "me."

The screenshot's analogy is: a person may feel personally affected when a virtual hand is struck; similarly, we experience the body's pleasure and pain as affecting ourselves. The confirmed theme adds the body/mind/consciousness distinction. Present this as the Tattva's teaching, illustrated through reflection; the prototype does not experimentally prove a metaphysical claim. See [the explanation and verified shlokas](tattva-explanation.md).

## The core explanation of Tattva

Explain these links in ordinary language:

1. **Movement:** I intend to move; the visible hand follows. This helps the user experience control, or agency.
2. **Ownership:** A hand that moves with me, appears in a plausible place, and receives corresponding cues may come to feel like "my hand." Agency and ownership are related but different experiences.
3. **An event:** Something touches that representation. With a verified motor, a bodily cue corresponds to the visible contact. Without a motor, only the visible interaction is demonstrated.
4. **Personal significance:** The participant may describe the event as happening to "me," despite knowing the representation is virtual. That report must come from the participant, not from our assumption.
5. **Awareness:** Ask the participant to notice both the hand and their thought/feeling about it. The Tattva distinguishes these known, changing contents from consciousness that knows them. This is a philosophical reflection, not an ESP measurement.

**Useful distinction:** the experience can be real to a participant even when the represented object is virtual. A visual event, actual motor sensation, interpretation of that event and physical injury are different things. No injury is needed for the demonstration.

Use the organizer's exact theme rather than assigning a school they did not name. Explain the verified Gita verses before connecting them to the demo. Do not say the program, headset or ESP is conscious.

### Research grounding, without overselling

Sanchez-Vives and colleagues' 2010 study found greater ownership reports during synchronous real/virtual hand movement than during the study's asynchronous condition. It supports our design rationale even before tactile output is completed; it does not establish that AHAM has reproduced the result. [Original study](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/80802/1/80802.pdf)

Another virtual-hand study examined how spatial context affected ownership responses. This supports treating ownership as something to assess rather than a guaranteed response. Our trial sheet is a brief informal feedback tool, not a validated ownership scale or replication of those experiments. [Original study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01659/full)

## A live experience that makes Tattva visible

Assign one teammate to guide the headset user and one to show the laptop/ESP. Keep narration short enough for the participant to concentrate.

Let a judge experience the hand directly when possible. If an actual headset view/recording is available, show it to spectators beside the three labels. The laptop's synthetic rehearsal is not a live mirror of the Quest hand; label it correctly. The receiver shows actual requests, not a participant's mental state.

1. **Orient:** "Open your right hand and turn your wrist slowly." Show the correspondence. Let the user bend fingers individually.
2. **Touch:** "Touch the light core." Show the index/contact request and actual board receipt. In the confirmed current setup, state that this is a request and no physical haptic cue is produced.
3. **Act:** "Pick up the core, lift it and open your hand over the matching color dock." Show that holding/release changes requests. Do not describe a virtual 3 kg label as a real 3 kg force.
4. **Reflect:** After the experience, ask: **"What did the hand feel like to you?"** Allow "just a model" or "no sensation" as valid responses.
5. **Connect:** Discuss identification using their actual answer, including control without ownership. Ask: "Can you notice the hand and also the thought you have about it?" Connect this to the Tattva's distinction between known body/mind contents and knowing consciousness.

Keep three visible cards beside the laptop: **BODY — represented hand; MIND — participant's own thought/feeling; AWARENESS — noticing both.** Open [judging-cards.html](judging-cards.html) locally in a browser, or print it; it works offline and sends no data or commands. The software shows the first, the participant reports the second, and the third is philosophical inquiry. No consciousness meter or inferred ownership score is used.

Optionally move the hand out of tracking view: the virtual representation becomes unavailable while the participant can still report their real hand and ongoing experience. This distinguishes the representation from the person experiencing it; it does not prove consciousness survives loss of the physical body.

The original brief asks for virtual contact/striking and a corresponding touch experience. The implemented game extends that brief but does not remove the need for a convincing contact cue. Explain the painless virtual contact as our demonstration adaptation; an external "strike the hand" event has not been specifically verified. Do not claim it is implemented if it is not.

Do not use a painful stimulus, surprise threat, or an untested servo pull to impress judges. If the current physical cue is missing, demonstrate the working tracking/data path and disclose the gap.

## Collect useful evidence without inventing results

Use [judging-observations.csv](judging-observations.csv). A few real trials can inform the presentation, but do not support population-level or causal claims.

- Observe whether the participant completes a touch/grab/lift/release and whether requests return to zero on release. Record failed attempts too.
- Ask an open question first: "What did you experience?" Then optionally rate control, ownership and contact sensation independently from 0 (not at all) to 5 (very strongly).
- Record hardware mode: requests only, verified vibration, or verified vibration plus resistance. Leave sensation ratings blank if not asked; do not replace missing answers with zero.
- Record only the participant's own words, with no names required. Do not lead them toward a desired Tattva answer.
- Report actual counts and raw ratings. Do not say "proved," "100% immersion," or "significant improvement" from a few informal trials.
- To compare haptics, both conditions must have working tracking and the same task; one uses a verified motor and the other does not. This is not the same comparison as synchronized versus asynchronous movement. No such comparator has been added to the website.

Do not create asynchronous tendon commands for a control condition. A future ownership study would need a defined protocol and suitable measures.

## Likely judge questions and concise answers

**How does this connect to Tattva rather than being a VR game?**

The hand may be treated as "mine." We distinguish the represented hand, the mind's thought/feeling of ownership, and awareness of both. This makes the body/mind/consciousness connection visible. The game holds attention; reflection establishes the conceptual link.

**Does the user actually feel touch?**

Currently no physical touch feedback is demonstrated. We show tracking and requests. A later verified coin motor would provide vibration as a contact cue, not realistic skin pressure.

**What is innovative if hand tracking and the illusion already exist?**

We combine optical hand tracking with a browser-based task, independent feedback requests and a locally controlled wireless glove prototype. Our contribution is the build and experience design. We acknowledge prior ownership research and do not claim to have discovered the illusion.

**Why remove flex sensors and MPU6050?**

Quest estimates finger joints and wrist pose, reducing the glove's sensing hardware and wiring. We reserve the glove for feedback. Optical tracking has occlusion limitations, so the actual fitted glove still requires a tracking check.

**What precisely works today?**

Both-hand Quest tracking and changing requests received by ESP are team-confirmed. State the actual tested motor/servo count at presentation time. Prepared code is described separately from verified physical output.

**Can the glove make a heavy virtual object weigh 3 kg?**

The virtual weight changes grip-resistance requests. A hand-mounted tendon may oppose finger motion after mechanical validation; it does not provide a calibrated 3 kg downward load on the arm.

**How do you know the virtual hand feels like their own?**

We ask users separately about control and ownership, and report their answers. Tracking accuracy or a successful game task alone does not prove ownership. Our informal feedback is preliminary.

**What does the display of RES% prove?**

It shows a computed request. An ESP receipt proves arrival; an applied pulse shows a command. Physical movement and force require separate observation/measurement.

**What is the practical impact?**

Our immediate target is an experiential teaching demonstration of perception and body identification. Success means learners can complete the task and explain the difference between a bodily cue, a virtual representation and personal identification. Medical or rehabilitation use would require additional work and validation.

**Does this prove the Self is separate from the body?**

It illustrates how bodily ownership and personal identification can be influenced. The philosophical interpretation follows the supplied Tattva; the prototype does not settle a metaphysical question.

**Can the GSR sensor prove the illusion or measure pain?**

GSR is not integrated into the selected demo. A physiological response alone would not identify ownership or pain. We keep actual participant reports separate from proposed future measurements.

**What if the judge does not feel ownership?**

That is a valid outcome. We report it and discuss tracking, fit, timing and individual variation as questions to investigate. We do not tell the participant what they must feel.

**How is the prototype controlled?**

The prepared actuator firmware starts disarmed, requires local arming and checks packet freshness. Servo travel and duration are bounded. Hardware power, force and release still require bench validation; disabling PWM alone does not guarantee tendon slack.

**Have you created consciousness in the ESP or software?**

No. We create an artificial body representation that may influence a human participant's sense of ownership. The electronics process pose and requests; they have no demonstrated subjective experience. Consciousness here concerns the participant and the supplied Tattva.
